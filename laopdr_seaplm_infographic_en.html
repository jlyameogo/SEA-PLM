Key message — widening inequalities
The gap between high and low achievers is widening — particularly in mathematics. Top performers gained +12 points at the 95th percentile between 2019 and 2024, while the weakest students (5th percentile) declined by 5 points. More resources must be directed to students in the lower half of the distribution.

Equity factors — observed score gaps
Language spoken at home
Lao-speaking students outscore non-Lao speakers by ~14 points in both domains. An estimated 40% of students in Lao PDR do not speak Lao at home.
Socioeconomic status
A ~21-point gap between the highest (Q4) and lowest (Q1) SES quartiles — equivalent to nearly 2 years of learning.
School location
Rural students in Lao PDR perform significantly below students in towns and cities in both reading and mathematics.
Early childhood education (ECE)
ECE attendance is linked to higher achievement. Access rose to 78% in 2024 (from 59% in 2019) but 22% still have no access.
Daily physical work
Students doing physical labour daily score significantly lower in both reading and mathematics.
Gender
Girls slightly outperform boys. 93% of boys do not reach the SDG reading threshold vs 86% of girls.
Recommendations by level of intervention
Family & early childhood
  • Expand access to at least 2 years of quality ECE, especially in rural and underserved areas
  • Target the 20% of children with no ECE access to achieve universal enrolment before primary school
  • Empower parents and caregivers in ethnolinguistic communities to support children's learning
  • Provide financial/social protection to reduce reliance on children's labour
Teachers
  • Expand in-service training in differentiated, inclusive and gender-responsive teaching
  • Provide targeted training on teaching Lao as a second language and multi-grade instruction
  • Train teachers in fostering reading motivation and comprehension strategies
  • Address unmet training needs in digital literacy, critical thinking and global citizenship
Schools & leadership
  • Develop structured mentoring and leadership programmes for new principals
  • Invest in classroom libraries, school reading programmes and structured reading time
  • Improve infrastructure where shortages significantly hinder teaching and learning
  • Manage classroom behavioural disruptions more effectively
Provincial & national government
  • Ensure teaching quality standards align with the national curriculum
  • Address shortages of qualified teachers in high-need areas
  • Review language-of-instruction policy to support non-Lao-speaking students
  • Direct more resources to students in the lower half of the achievement distribution
Overall message
SEA-PLM 2024 results show that learning inequalities remain deep in Lao PDR. Despite modest gains in mathematics, a large majority of Grade 5 students have not yet acquired the foundational skills expected at the end of primary school. The most persistent drivers — language of instruction, socioeconomic background, rural location, and limited ECE access — require targeted, equity-focused policies sustained across all levels of the education system.
